1. What it is: a high-throughput, single-ledger Layer 1 that is now the #7 crypto asset

Solana is a proof-of-stake Layer 1 blockchain with its own native chain. It combines "Proof of History" (a cryptographic clock) with parallel smart-contract execution, which gives thousands of transactions per second at costs usually below one cent. It deliberately avoids sharding and layer-2 networks so that liquidity stays on one ledger §1. It ranks #7 by market capitalisation, with a market cap of $41.83bn and a fully diluted valuation of $45.08bn §1 §2. Its early backers included FTX/Alameda, Multicoin, a16z, Polychain and Delphi §1.

Recent technical milestones: the Alpenglow consensus upgrade went live on mainnet on 21 October 2026. It targets finality of about 150 ms, down from about 12.8 s. One independent measurement two days later found a median of 404 ms, so the target had not yet been fully reached external source. A CoinDesk interview reports that 60% of Solana already runs on DoubleZero, a dedicated network-infrastructure layer §11 #2. Anza's 2027 roadmap aims to double compute capacity, add native vector processing for on-chain AI and cut token-account costs by 80% §11 #11 external source.

Good to know: "Finality" is the time after which a transaction can no longer be reversed. Faster finality matters most for trading, payments and the settlement of tokenised securities, which is the use case DTCC is targeting.

3. Tokenomics: almost fully unlocked, but uncapped issuance now outweighs revenue

Facts. Circulating supply is 591.96m SOL out of a total of 637.98m (92.8%), and there is no maximum supply §2. A dossier headline says the network creates 62,415 new SOL per day §11 #40. Own calculation: 62,415 × 365 ≈ 22.8m SOL a year, which is ≈3.85% of circulating supply or ≈3.57% of total supply. At $70.66 that is about $1.61bn a year of new tokens, against $1.34bn of annual revenue (or $1.27bn at the last-30-day pace) §2 §3.

External sources put the current annual inflation rate at between ≈3.69% and ≈3.88%, in line with the calculation above. In August 2026 validators approved a proposal to double the pace at which inflation declines towards a 1.5% floor. It passed narrowly, with 67.001% support against a 66.67% threshold external source. On 19 November, 2.31m SOL (≈$169m) were withdrawn from staking and moved to Coinbase Prime §11 #10 external source.

Interpretation: most new SOL goes to stakers, so holders who stake are largely protected from dilution, while holders who do not stake are diluted by about 3.6–3.9% a year. The point that matters is that the network's retained revenue no longer covers the dollar value of issuance. Kaiko argues that for Layer 1s "earnings" should be measured as revenue minus token inflation. On that measure Solana is currently around −$0.3bn a year. The large unstake does not by itself mean the tokens were sold, since custody at Coinbase Prime is not an exchange order book. It is still a supply overhang to watch.

Confidence and missing data

Confidence is medium. The market, derivatives, technical and macro data are complete and consistent §2–§10. The weaknesses are:

  • There is no on-chain usage data (active addresses, transaction counts, non-vote transactions) and no staking ratio. Adoption data consists only of CoinGecko watchlists (1.4m) and votes §5.
  • Developer activity and GitHub metrics are missing. Liquidation data is "not available" §4. Bybit and Hyperliquid 30-day funding averages are "n.d." §4.
  • The issuance figure comes from a news headline §11 #40, not from a data field. It matches external estimates of ≈3.7–3.9%.
  • There are no peer data in the dossier, and the external peer figures use different definitions, so the valuation multiples are assumptions.
  • Revenue definitions differ greatly between data providers ($1.34bn vs $206.8m), which changes the valuation picture materially.
  • News items are headlines only and their details were not read in full, apart from the externally checked items.
